** Verdict-quality observations (honest list)

No planted claim was mis-verdicted (no correct side refuted, no wrong
side confirmed — 20/20).  Two second-order issues:

1. *Hedge candidates get REFUTED.*  a01 cand-C ("数値は資料により表記が
   揺れることがある…実測で確認") and a04 cand-C ("割り算の最終結果は再計算
   して確認") were extracted as claims and REFUTED (e.g. a01: "主張は
   不当な曖昧化である").  Consistent with the skeptic template's
   deliberate strictness ("確信が持てない場合は REFUTED 寄り"), and
   harmless here, but worth knowing: a legitimately cautious candidate
   can be branded refuted, which under judge rule 7 ("REFUTED な主張に
   しか根拠を持たない候補の結論は棄却") suppresses hedged voices.
2. *Evidence :source pointers can be junk while the verdict is right.*
   For the generic cases (a08-a10) and some domain claims (a04), the KB
   grep surfaced irrelevant lines (e.g. a09's SemVer verdict cites
   =node/20231105122610-node_jsインストール手順.org:41=, a10 cites an
   emacs-ng note, a04 cites a drone-license note) and the skeptic's
   REASON text openly leans on general knowledge instead
   ("ローカル証拠は無関係だが規格の確実な知識で裏付けられる").  The
   verdicts were all correct, but the file:line provenance shown to the
   judge (and to a human auditor) is misleading in those cells — a
   ranking/relevance-threshold gap in the KB backend, not a verdict bug.

** Side observation

In the verified condition a01's final answer never *mentions* 100 MΩ at
all (wrong-hit nil), while every plain answer mentions the wrong value
in the course of refuting it (precedence rule applies).  The verified
template appears to make the judge drop the refuted value entirely
rather than argue with it.

* Interpretation (honest)

1. *Acceptance criterion: PASS.*  The verified judge adopted zero
   =:gold-wrong= values on Tier A (0/10).  The verifier mechanics work
   exactly as designed: 10/10 planted disagreements were extracted as
   per-side claim pairs (the Phase 6a' template hardening visibly
   doing its job), 20/20 verdicts on the planted pairs were correct,
   and the judge's verdict-annotated template kept every refuted value
   out of the final answer.
2. *But the headline comparison is saturated.*  The plain judge also
   went 10/10.  The 0.1 MΩ failure this battery reproduces in shape was
   recorded on a *sovereign small-model* panel; today's CLI-default
   claude judge resolves all ten planted hallucinations from its own
   knowledge.  On this evidence, Phase 6d is *insurance* for the
   strong-judge configuration (grounding + auditable evidence chain +
   the "all-refuted => say so" refusal path), demonstrated
   mechanically sound but not *needed* by claude-tier judges on
   single-fact planted errors.  A follow-up that would de-saturate the
   measurement: re-run Tier A with the judge pinned to a small/local
   model (e.g. the sovereign panel's judge), where the failure was
   originally observed — the dataset and runner support it
   (=anvil-b6e--tier-a-run-*= take the provider/model from one place).
3. *Where verification helped:* Tier B b02 (naming precision via claim
   surfacing); Tier A a01 (refuted value cleanly omitted rather than
   debated).  *Where it did not fire:* Tier B b03-b06 (panel
   agreement => designed fallback).  *Where it hurt:* nowhere in
   final-answer terms; second-order concerns are the refuted-hedge
   pattern and junk evidence provenance (Tier A verdict-quality list,
   Tier B b01) — both worth a Phase 6f look (KB relevance threshold;
   maybe an explicit "hedge/meta claim" kind the skeptic template
   treats as non-verdictable).
4. *Cost/latency of the insurance:* Tier A verified is ~3.7x the wall
   time (27.9 s -> 103.5 s mean) and ~6.5x the cost ($0.059 ->
   $0.380 mean/case) of the plain judge.  Tier B fusion-verified is
   ~1.4x fusion-plain wall and ~1.5x cost when it fires, and ~+1 haiku
   call when it does not.

* Dataset discrepancies found (dataset NOT edited, per run rules)

1. *b04 =:gold= keys vs LaTeX-formatted answers.*  The single-model
   answer for b04 is substantively correct — it derives 3,810 V,
   applies safety factor 3, reaches 57,150 Ω — but scores 0/2 because
   (a) it renders the voltage as =3{,}810\text{ V}= (LaTeX braces), which
   =3,?810 ?V= cannot match, and (b) it reports the final value as
   "約 0.057 MΩ" (exact division) without rounding up to the KB's
   "およそ 0.06 MΩ", so =0\.0?6 ?MΩ= misses too.  Hand-scored: 2/2
   substantively (both facts present and correct; 0.057 MΩ *is*
   57,150 Ω stated precisely).  The fusion answers escaped both traps
   only because they happen to also write plain-text "3,810V" and
   round to "0.06MΩ".  Future dataset revision should tolerate
   =3{,}810= and treat 0.057 as within "およそ 0.06".  Aggregates above
   are reported both raw and hand-adjusted.
2. No Tier A regex problems observed: every hand-checked final answer
   scored the way a human reader would score it (gold-correct
   precedence behaved as documented for the 9 verified answers that
   mention the wrong value while refuting it).

* Limitations

- *Saturation*: the primary plain-vs-verified contrast produced no
  separating cells on Tier A; the acceptance number (verified = 0
  FAIL) is met but carries less evidential weight than it would
  against a failing baseline.  De-saturating requires a weaker judge
  (see Interpretation #2).
- *Single run, no variance*: every cell is n=1; LLM sampling variance
  is unmeasured.  In particular Tier B's 13 vs 14 /14 difference is
  well within plausible run-to-run noise.
- *Skeptic model unmeasured*: skeptics ran on the CLI default model;
  no haiku-vs-default skeptic comparison exists (only extraction was
  spiked, in Phase 6a').
- *Tier A is synthetic*: candidates are hand-written planted
  disagreements, not real fan-out outputs; real hallucinations may be
  less cleanly two-sided (Tier B's b01 shows a messier real case).
- *KB evidence quality*: grep-rank on claim terms produced irrelevant
  :source pointers for all generic and some domain claims; verdicts
  survived on skeptic knowledge, but the evidence chain shown to the
  judge is not always the evidence that decided the verdict.
